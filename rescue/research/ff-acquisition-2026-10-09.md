# Fluent Forever audience acquisition — 2026-10-09

## Decision

Target learners who already believe in learning sounds first and now need something usable to practice with. Offer an immediate listening check and a small useful lesson, not another explanation of ABX methodology. The opportunity is a focused companion/alternative for sound training, not a claim to replace Fluent Forever's whole language-learning app. No competitor traffic, keyword-volume or conversion data was available in this research; proposed search intents are hypotheses, not measured demand.

Its [minimal-pairs article](https://blog.fluent-forever.com/learning-pronunciation/) connects the sound-learning argument to its app. These are direct evidence of the audience's vocabulary and journey, not evidence of its size.

## AI discovery

Make the first paragraph answer what the tool does, whom it helps, which languages are actually playable, what is free and what costs money. Put prices, limitations and accessible text examples in rendered HTML. Link a useful comparison/method page and original demonstrations; keep canonical URLs, internal links and sitemap coherent. This creates answerable, verifiable source material, not guaranteed recommendations.

Google's [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) emphasizes standard search eligibility and useful content. Its newer [generative AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) also describes Search Console controls for inclusion: inspect the actual account's available setting rather than assume old guidance or universal rollout. Audit relevant crawler access, but crawler allowance alone does not prove indexing/recommendation. No claims that adding an llms.txt file, keyword repetition or schema buys AI placement. Ask willing buyers how they found us; preserve coarse known referrer categories when consent permits. Unknown attribution remains unknown.

## Feedback and optional after-error hints

**Evidence:** [Shinohara and Iverson2018](https://y.shinohara.w.waseda.jp/assets/files/Shinohara_and_Iverson_2018.pdf) compared identification and discrimination for Japanese learners of English /r–l/. Identification replayed the target after feedback; discrimination replayed the sequence on errors. Both improved learning similarly in that setting. It does not establish universal ABX superiority or the best replay policy across languages. Their discrimination tasks are not identical to our game.

The [recent HVPT meta-analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6) defines included training through multiple voices/contexts plus immediate feedback; feedback-free studies were excluded. Therefore its positive aggregate result is not by itself a randomized proof that feedback is necessary or that explanations outperform replay.

[Lee and Lyster2016](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12167) directly compared corrective-feedback variants and no feedback in100 Korean learners, targeting English vowels. The retrieved abstract establishes the design, but not enough result detail here to recommend a specific explanation format as proven superior. Written articulatory hints are not equivalent to that paper's visual correctness feedback.

**Product recommendation, an inference to test:** retain automatic correct-sound replay during training. After an error, offer a small optional “What to listen for” expansion, pausing auto-advance only when opened. One precise contrast-specific cue, two example words plus IPA, and replay controls; no long lecture or forced text before an answer. Start with manually checked /u–y/ and /ɪ–i/ guidance. Never simplify /ɪ–i/ to duration alone. Do not add instructional hints or corrective replay during scored diagnostic trials; explanation can appear after completion.

Evaluate hints against replay-only using new-word/new-speaker transfer, later-session retention, completion and customer reports. Immediate repeat-item improvement can be memorization. At present traffic, use usability observations and descriptive counts; do not announce statistical uplift from a handful of users.
