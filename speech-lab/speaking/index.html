How to read the map

Each point describes a short, central part of the recorded sound. The chart uses two vowel resonances, F1 and F2. Points closer together have more similar measurements, but that alone cannot tell us whether the word was pronounced correctly.

Compare your own attempts using the same microphone. Example recordings are labelled separately: another speaker naturally has a different vowel space. The displayed word is the prompt you selected, not a word the system detected.

If the measurements disagree, no point is shown for that attempt. The background check helps reject noisy microphone recordings; uploaded files and examples do not have that check. Some processed or noisy audio can still pass.

Recording asks the browser to turn off echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain. Devices may still process audio. The blue live dot uses a faster, less reliable tracker than the after-recording measurement. It does not classify the sound as ship or sheep. Live practice pauses after one minute or when the page is hidden.
